This appendix derives the one-dimensional interband emission (and absorption) integral near the **L point** in gold's Brillouin zone, following the same machinery used in [[A8 - Interband Transitions (Rosei)]] for the X point. The difference from X is a single sign, and it is the whole story: at X the upper band is a **saddle** (up in the face, down along $\Gamma X$), whereas at L the upper band $L_6^-$ is a **minimum** — it curves **up** in *both* directions. That one flip changes the sign of $\bar B$, the sign of the determinant $D$, and moves the integrable singularity from the upper edge (X) to the lower edge (L). This is exactly the "splitting of the interband edge" that Guerrisi–Rosei (1975) describe: X gives a soft $M_1$ shoulder, L a sharp $M_0$ onset.

# L-Point Dispersion Relations

Near the L point the bands are approximated as

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E}_{l}(\mathbf{k})&=-\mathcal{E}_{0l} - A_{l}k_{\perp}^{2} - B_{l}k_{\parallel}^{2}  &&(\mathcal{E}\le-\mathcal{E}_{0l})\\ \\
\mathcal{E}_{u}(\mathbf{k})&= \ \ \mathcal{E}_{0u} + A_{u}k_{\perp}^{2} \boldsymbol{+} B_{u}k_{\parallel}^{2} &&(\mathcal{E}\ge\mathcal{E}_{0u})
\end{align}
$$

with all coefficients $A_{i}=\hbar^{2}/2m_{i\perp}$, $B_{i}=\hbar^{2}/2m_{i\parallel} > 0$ ($i=u,l$). The only change from the X-point dispersions is the **$+B_{u}k_{\parallel}^{2}$** in the upper band (at X it was $-B_{u}k_{\parallel}^{2}$). Christensen & Seraphin give $m_{u\parallel}^{L}=+0.12$: positive mass along $\Gamma L$, so the upper band is a genuine **minimum**, not a saddle. The lower ($d$) band is unchanged — a maximum in both directions.

The starting point is the same direct-transition integral

$$
\Gamma_{e}^{\text{ul}}(\hbar\omega) \propto\int\limits_{\text{BZ}} d^{3}k \; f(\mathcal{E}_{u}(\mathbf{k})) \Big[1-f(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\mathbf{k}))\Big] \; \delta\Big(\mathcal{E}_{u}(\mathbf{k}) - \mathcal{E}_{l}(\mathbf{k}) - \hbar\omega  \Big)
$$

and we proceed through the same sequence of changes of variables.

# Cylindrical Symmetry

Identical to the X-point case:

$$
d^{3}k \to 2\pi k_{\perp}\, dk_{\perp}\, dk_{\parallel}
$$

# Linearization of Momenta

Identical substitution — naming the squared momenta $x = k_{\perp}^{2}$, $y = k_{\parallel}^{2}$ (to avoid clashing with the band labels), with the factor-of-two symmetry accounting for $\pm k_{\parallel}$:

$$
2\pi k_{\perp}\, dk_{\perp}\, dk_{\parallel} \;\to\; \pi \frac{dx\,dy}{\sqrt{y}}
$$

In these variables the dispersions become **linear**:

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E}_{u}(x,y) &= \mathcal{E}_{0u} + A_{u}x + B_{u}y \\
\mathcal{E}_{l}(x,y) &= -\mathcal{E}_{0l} - A_{l}x - B_{l}y
\end{align}
$$

Note that $x$ and $y$ now enter $\mathcal{E}_u$ with the **same (positive) sign** — the signature of the minimum, and the one place the L algebra parts from X.

# Energy-Space Transformation

We introduce the same physically motivated variables

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E} &\equiv \mathcal{E}_{u}(x,y) &&\implies\quad \mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{0u} = A_{u}x + B_{u}y \\
\Delta &\equiv \mathcal{E}_{u}(x,y) - \mathcal{E}_{l}(x,y) &&\implies\quad \Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g} = \bar{A}\,x + \bar{B}\,y
\end{align}
$$

where

$$
\mathcal{E}_{g} \equiv \mathcal{E}_{0u} + \mathcal{E}_{0l}, \qquad \bar{A} \equiv A_{u} + A_{l}, \qquad \bar{B} \equiv B_{l} + B_{u}
$$

> **Different from X.** Because the upper band contributes $+B_{u}y$, we get $\bar{B}^{L} = B_{l} + B_{u}$ — a **sum**, always positive (at X it was the difference $B_{l}-B_{u}$). Both coefficients of $\Delta-\mathcal{E}_g$ are now positive, so **$\Delta(x,y)$ is a minimum** ($M_0$). At X, $\bar B_X<0$ makes $\Delta$ a saddle ($M_1$) — that is the physical difference between the two edges.

## Matrix Form

$$
\begin{pmatrix} \mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{0u} \\ \Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g} \end{pmatrix}
= \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} A_{u} & +B_{u} \\ \bar{A} & \bar{B} \end{pmatrix}}_{M^{L}}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}
$$

The top-right entry is $+B_{u}$ (at X it was $-B_{u}$).

**Here is $D$.** The Jacobean is the determinant of $M^{L}$,

$$
D^{L} \equiv \det(M^{L}) = A_{u}\bar{B} - B_{u}\bar{A} = A_{u}B_{l} - A_{l}B_{u} .
$$

> **Sign of $D^{L}$.** Unlike at X (where $D^{X}=A_uB_l+A_lB_u>0$ always), here $D^{L}=A_uB_l-A_lB_u$ is a **difference**. For gold's L masses it is **negative**, $D^{L}<0$. Its sign is what flips the singular edge below; we carry $|D^{L}|$ under the roots.

## Inverse Transformation

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}
= \frac{1}{D^{L}}
\begin{pmatrix} \bar{B} & -B_{u} \\ -\bar{A} & A_{u} \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} \mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{0u} \\ \Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g} \end{pmatrix}
$$

giving

$$
\begin{align}
x(\mathcal{E}, \Delta) &= \frac{1}{D^{L}}\Big[\bar{B}(\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{0u}) - B_{u}(\Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g})\Big] \\[6pt]
y(\mathcal{E}, \Delta) &= \frac{1}{D^{L}}\Big[-\bar{A}(\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{0u}) + A_{u}(\Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g})\Big]
\end{align}
$$

## Integration Limits

Impose $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$. Because $D^{L}<0$, each inequality flips when we clear the $1/D^{L}$ — and that flip **swaps which constraint gives which edge** relative to X.

From $y \geq 0$ (the **lower** edge at L):

$$
\mathcal{E} \geq \mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{A_{u}}{A_{u} + A_{l}}(\Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g}) \equiv \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\Delta)
$$

From $x \geq 0$ (the **upper** edge at L):

$$
\mathcal{E} \leq \mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{B_{u}}{B_{l} + B_{u}}(\Delta - \mathcal{E}_{g}) \equiv \mathcal{E}_{max}^{L}(\Delta)
$$

> **Opposite to X.** At X, $y\geq0$ set the *upper* edge and $x\geq0$ the *lower* one; here it is reversed. Both edges are geometric and both sit **above** $\mathcal{E}_{0u}$ (proportional to $\Delta-\mathcal{E}_g\geq0$): a **closed** window, characteristic of the $M_0$ minimum. At X the window opens downward and its floor is set by occupation, not geometry.

## The Measure

Express $y$ through $\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}$ (note $A_{u}(\Delta-\mathcal{E}_g)=\bar A(\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}-\mathcal{E}_{0u})$):

$$
y(\mathcal{E}, \Delta) = \frac{\bar{A}}{D^{L}}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\Delta) - \mathcal{E}\Big) = \frac{\bar{A}}{|D^{L}|}\Big(\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\Delta)\Big)
$$

which is manifestly non-negative in the allowed range $\mathcal{E} \geq \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}$ (using $D^{L}<0$ in the second step). Inserting into the measure:

$$
\pi \frac{dx\,dy}{\sqrt{y}} \;\to\; \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\bar{A}\,|D^{L}|}} \; \frac{d\Delta\, d\mathcal{E}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\Delta)}}
$$

> **Singularity flipped from X.** The root is now $1/\sqrt{\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}}$, diverging at the **lower** integration limit (at X it was $1/\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{max}-\mathcal{E}}$, at the *upper* limit). The $k_{\parallel}=0$ circle ($y=0$) maps to the **bottom** of the energy window here, pinning the van Hove singularity right at the onset — this is what makes the L edge *sharp*.

# Resolving the Delta Function

Assembling the full integral with the measure and resolving $\delta(\Delta - \hbar\omega)$, with $\mathcal{E}_{l} = \mathcal{E} - \hbar\omega$:

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{e}^{\text{ul},L}(\hbar\omega) = \frac{\pi^{2}|\mu|^{2}}{\hbar\sqrt{\bar{A}\,|D^{L}|}}
\int\limits_{\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\hbar\omega)}^{\mathcal{E}_{max}^{L}(\hbar\omega)}
\frac{f(\mathcal{E})\Big[1-f(\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega)\Big]}{\sqrt{\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\hbar\omega)}}
\; d\mathcal{E}
}
$$

with

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}(\hbar\omega) &= \mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{A_{u}}{A_{u}+A_{l}}\,(\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_{g}) \\[4pt]
\mathcal{E}_{max}^{L}(\hbar\omega) &= \mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{B_{u}}{B_{l}+B_{u}}\,(\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_{g})
\end{align}
$$

# Where $F$ is: Rosei's mass factor at L

As at X, the masses enter the prefactor only through the determinant, packaged into the mass factor $F$ (Rosei's Eq. 5). Here it carries $|D^{L}|$:

$$
F^{L} \equiv \frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\sqrt{|D^{L}|}} = \left( \left|\frac{1}{m_{u\perp}m_{l\parallel}} - \frac{1}{m_{l\perp}m_{u\parallel}}\right| \right)^{-1/2}.
$$

The mass-explicit form has a **difference** under the root (at X it was a sum), the direct fingerprint of the minimum topology — and it is exactly why $D^{L}$ can, and for gold does, go negative. As before $\sqrt{|D^{L}|}=\hbar^{2}/2F^{L}$, so $1/\sqrt{\bar A\,|D^{L}|}=2F^{L}/(\hbar^{2}\sqrt{\bar A})$.

# Comparison with the X-Point Result

The two critical points are **not** the same — the single sign flip in the upper band propagates through the whole derivation:

| | X point (A8) | L point (this note) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Upper band | $\mathcal{E}_{0u} + A_u u - B_u v$ (saddle) | $\mathcal{E}_{0u} + A_u u + B_u v$ (minimum) |
| $\bar{B}$ | $B_l - B_u$ (can be $<0$) | $B_l + B_u > 0$ |
| $D$ | $A_u B_l + A_l B_u > 0$ | $A_u B_l - A_l B_u < 0$ |
| $\Delta(x,y)$ | saddle ($M_1$) | minimum ($M_0$) |
| Singularity | $1/\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{max} - \mathcal{E}}$ (**upper** limit) | $1/\sqrt{\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{min}}$ (**lower** limit) |
| Window | opens downward; floor from occupation | closed; both edges geometric |
| $\mathcal{E}_{min}$ | $\mathcal{E}_{0u} - \frac{B_u}{B_l - B_u}(\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_g)$ | $\mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{A_u}{A_u + A_l}(\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_g)$ |
| $\mathcal{E}_{max}$ | $\mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{A_u}{A_u + A_l}(\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_g)$ | $\mathcal{E}_{0u} + \frac{B_u}{B_l + B_u}(\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_g)$ |
| Edge shape | soft shoulder | sharp threshold |

# Verification: JDOS at $T = 0$

At zero temperature the Fermi factors become step functions. For $\hbar\omega$ just above threshold the whole window $[\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}, \mathcal{E}_{max}^{L}]$ is available, and the integral reduces to

$$
J^{L}(\hbar\omega) \propto \int_{\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}}^{\mathcal{E}_{max}^{L}} \frac{d\mathcal{E}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}}} = 2\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{max}^{L} - \mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}} \propto \sqrt{\hbar\omega - \mathcal{E}_g}
$$

since $\mathcal{E}_{max}^{L}-\mathcal{E}_{min}^{L}=\big(\tfrac{B_u}{B_l+B_u}-\tfrac{A_u}{A_u+A_l}\big)(\hbar\omega-\mathcal{E}_g)$. This is the textbook $M_0$ onset — a clean square-root rise from zero, with the singular (lower) edge pinned at $\mathcal{E}_g$. Both the individual upper band and the difference surface $\Delta(x,y)$ are minima here, so nothing smears the threshold: the L edge is **sharp**, unlike the occupation-limited X edge.

# Note on Absorption vs Emission

For **emission** (upper → lower): the Fermi factor is $f(\mathcal{E})[1-f(\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega)]$.

For **absorption** ($\varepsilon_2$, lower → upper): the Fermi factor is $f(\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega)[1-f(\mathcal{E})]$.

At equilibrium these are related by detailed balance: $\text{emission}/\text{absorption} = e^{-\beta\hbar\omega}$.
