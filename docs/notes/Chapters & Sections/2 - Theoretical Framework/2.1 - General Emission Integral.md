For the electronic aspect of emission, the most general expression describes the transition of an electron from a state with momentum $\mathbf{k}_{1}$ in energy band $n$ to a state with momentum $\mathbf{k}_{2}$ in energy band $m$ is as follows (see [A1 - Derivation of the General Emission Integral](../A%20-%20Appendices/A1%20-%20Derivation%20of%20the%20General%20Emission%20Integral.md))

$$
\Gamma_{e}(\hbar\omega) = \sum_{n, m} \iint\limits_{\text{BZ}} |\mu^{nm}(\mathbf{k_{1}},\mathbf{k_{2}})|^{2} \ f_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1})\overline{f_{m}(\mathbf{k}_{2})} \ \delta\big(\mathcal{E}_{m}(\mathbf{k}_{2}) - \mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbf{k}_{1}) + \hbar\omega \big) \ d^{3}k_{1} \ d^{3}k_{2}
$$

This is gained from FGR integrated over all initial and final states and weighted by electronic distribution which reflect occupancy $f$ and vacancy $\overline{f}$ in the initial and final states respectively. Electronic distribution can be either thermal (Fermi-Dirac) $f^{T}$, steady-state $f^{S}$ or transient $f^{P}$ for CW and pulsed illumination (more on those later). 

The transition dipole moment $\mu^{nm}$ is generally $\mathbf{k}$ and energy dependent. For interband transitions it is a common approximation to consider the TDM as constant. In the case of intraband transitions in the conduction band this is not necessarily the case. The second order TDM required for intraband transitions in bulk metals has explicit energy dependence. In nano-particles however, spatial confinement results in momentum uncertainty which opens a first order channel for intraband transition that is, to a good approximation (according to Yonatan), has weak energy dependence and can be considered constant. We chose to work with the latter case and therefore, in all of our emission integrals the TDM is taken out of the integral as constant.
