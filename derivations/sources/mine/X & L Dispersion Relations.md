Energy conservation for 

$$
\begin{cases}
&\text{emission:}&&\mathcal{E}_{\text{c}}(\mathbf{k}_{1})=\mathcal{E}_{\text{v}}(\mathbf{k}_{2}) + \hbar\omega \\ \\
&\text{absorption:}&&\mathcal{E}_{\text{c}}(\mathbf{k}_{2})=\mathcal{E}_{\text{v}}(\mathbf{k}_{1}) + \hbar\omega
\end{cases}
$$

 Dispersion relation for the L point is as follows

$$
\mathcal{E}^{\text{L}}(\mathbf{k}) = \begin{cases}
\mathcal{E}_{\text{c}}^{\text{L}}(\mathbf{k}) &= +\mathcal{E}_{\text{c0}}^{\text{L}} + A_{\text{c}}^{\text{L}}k_{\perp}^{2}\textcolor{#E76F51}{\boldsymbol{+}} B_{\text{c}}^{\text{L}}
k_{\parallel}^{2} &&\mathcal{E^{\text{L}}}>+\mathcal{E_{\text{c0}}}\\
\mathcal{E}_{\text{v}}^{\text{L}}(\mathbf{k}) &= -\mathcal{E}_{\text{v0}}^{\text{L}} - A_{\text{v}}^{\text{L}}k_{\perp}^{2} - B_{\text{v}}^{\text{L}}
k_{\parallel}^{2} &&\mathcal{E^{\text{L}}}<-\mathcal{E_{\text{v0}}}
\end{cases}
$$

Dispersion relation for the X point is as follows
$$
\mathcal{E}^{\text{X}}(\mathbf{k}) = \begin{cases}
\mathcal{E}_{\text{c}}^{\text{X}}(\mathbf{k}) &= +\mathcal{E}_{\text{c0}}^{\text{X}} + A_{\text{c}}^{\text{X}}k_{\perp}^{2} \textcolor{#E76F51}{\boldsymbol{-}} B_{\text{c}}^{\text{X}}
k_{\parallel}^{2} &&\mathcal{E^{\text{X}}}>+\mathcal{E_{\text{c0}}}\\
\mathcal{E}_{\text{v}}^{\text{X}}(\mathbf{k}) &= -\mathcal{E}_{\text{v0}}^{\text{X}} - A_{\text{v}}^{\text{X}}k_{\perp}^{2}- B_{\text{v}}^{\text{X}}
k_{\parallel}^{2} &&\mathcal{E^{\text{X}}}<-\mathcal{E_{\text{v0}}}
\end{cases}
$$

# Emission VS Absorption

```
E_c -> E_v + hw (emission)
E_c <- E_v + hw (absorption)
```

In both cases CEDS is 
$$
\Omega^{P}(\mathbf{k}) = \mathcal{E}^{P}_{c}(\mathbf{k})-\mathcal{E}^{P}_{v}(\mathbf{k})
$$
what actually dictates the direction is the electron occupation factors

```
f(E_c)[1 - f(E_v)] (emission)
f(E_v)[1 - f(E_c)] (eabsorption)
```
